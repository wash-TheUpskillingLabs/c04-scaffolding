Phase 2: Group Convergence at Meet the Pod

At Meet the Pod, you’ll bring your individual work as a lightning talk. We’ll spend the rest of the
evening starting to bring this work back together to the group. You’ll cluster your individual
research and hypothesis together to start to define a handful of design paradoxes for the
group.

      The Paradox Formula:
      A paradox is a contradiction that the current way of thinking can't resolve. Pursuing what
      you want produces the opposite, and the standard ways of pursuing it harder make it
      worse. Naming the paradox clearly is what opens the door to a fresh frame.

      Some examples:

           ●​ The Sydney Opera House is beloved because people can walk right up to
              it. But that same closeness is what lets people graffiti the tiles. Build a
              fence to stop the graffiti and you destroy the closeness that made it
              beloved.
           ●​ Public meetings work when everyone can speak, because that's what makes
              them democratic. Public meetings also work when decisions actually get made,
              because that's what makes them worth attending. But the more voices you let in,
              the harder it is to decide anything — and the faster you decide, the more voices
              you've cut out.
           ●​ Test scores were introduced to hold schools accountable for teaching. But
              because scores are the metric, schools narrow instruction to what's tested and
              drill for the format, so scores rise while actual learning stays flat or declines.
​
      Here’s a rough formula for writing a paradox:
          ●​ The value. Here is what is supposed to happen or is working and why it matters.
          ●​ The failure. Here is how that same element causes the problem.
          ●​ The trap. Here’s why an obvious fix destroys the core value.
Assignment 2: Generate a Frame Hypothesis

  ●​ Due Date: August 14 (Pre-Hackathon)
  ●​ The Big Question: If we look at the problem differently , and shift or clarify who
      encounters the problem , what would we build for them?
Assignment 1 named what's stuck and why. Assignment 2 breaks the stuckness by changing
the lens you’re looking at the problem through: the “frame.”

The frame you choose decides everything downstream: If you see low civic engagement as an
information problem, you build a website. See it as a belonging problem and you build
something else entirely, for entirely different people.

The hackathon helps you build the things to test your frame. Your job in Assignment 2 is to give
the hackathon a frame to build against, and a real stakeholder worth building for.
