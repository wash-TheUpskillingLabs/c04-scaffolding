# Interview: an organizer on structure, what Upskillers needed, and how Cycles 1 and 2 changed

Filed as issue #28 (Nov 14, 2026).

1. What it is
A conversation to understand an organizer's thoughts on scaffolding: what
she thinks about structure, what she has observed Upskillers needed, and
how things changed over the Cycles.

2. Where it came from
An organizer. She asked to be described as "an organizer" and nothing
more, and to speak about Cycles 1 and 2 as an organizer, not about her
own experience as a participant. Facts about the
pilot appear as "an organizer described the pilot as...".

3. When
Nov 9, 2026 (about 70 minutes, by video). Speaker labels in the

4. What it says
(Quotes are verbatim from the conversation, lightly trimmed with "...",
with filler words such as "like" and "you know" removed where they don't
change the meaning. She asked to speak as an organizer about Cycles 1 and
2, not about her own experience as a participant.)

Changing the model mid-Cycle
- "The model is changing as participants were going through it." In early
  Cycles, "every day something new happened or new idea came up. Literally
  every day." Examples: "oh wait, we need a QR code for this presentation,
  or oh wait, are we going to do pods or projects, or wait, are we going to
  have people vote on this."
- "Some people wanted the model to stay consistent for a build cycle
  without adapting it. But there wasn't clear agreement not to try some new
  tweak, because we're moving really fast."
- Her recommendation: "Here's the plan for this cycle. We're not going to
  adjust it. We're just going to see what happens. And then adjust it the
  next cycle... but not midstream. Because you can't really evaluate
  anything if you're constantly changing it, because you don't know what
  variable made it work or not work."
- On grants: "when we change the model, our applications are just like...
  I need some consistency even in the program model."
- "My main thing is, don't change the cycle halfway through."

What participants needed: what's next
- "Participants needed to know what was next, what to expect to do."
- "We tended not to communicate upfront... in a way that was clear
  enough... that we were all going to come
  in and work to research a problem, and that we might end up not with our
  own problem, but another problem... And the path to get there was not
  clear."
- "There's no clear timeline... on this date you're going to do this...
  As much as we tried to develop a syllabus, we couldn't because things
  kept changing."
- The "What to Expect" guide: "People didn't really read it. It was too
  much information, I think, and it didn't really make sense to them." On
  the guide today: "I don't like it very much." The chart in it was added
  later; "there's not enough detail. I don't know what problem voting looks
  like."
- Steps that aren't explained: "where do the proposed problems come from?
  Who generates the proposed problems? How does the voting happen?"
- Late requirements for presentations: "oh, you need to put together a
  logo, or oh, you need to put together a QR code, or oh, you need your
  PowerPoint." Examples from past Cycles would help, with a caveat:
  "sometimes people aren't as creative as they could be... it becomes a
  little bit more rote. But some people do need things to look at."
- An orientation: "Do the kickoff and follow that with an orientation,
  maybe," since the material is "pretty complicated."

The timeline
- The dashboard timeline isn't clickable: "that's my point. But if it
  was... you could click on week one, and you could see, this week we're
  doing this, here's the workshops, this is how this relates."
- What she'd want behind each week: "What are the goals of week one, what
  are we gonna achieve? ... these are the workshops that are going to be
  available. And this is how it relates to what I'm trying to do." Plus "a
  preview of what week 2 is. So you can sort of see a week ahead."
- Sprints: two organizers designed them, and "they were supposed to be 2
  weeks... this is what you're gonna accomplish in these 2 weeks." (She
  "didn't get in the weeds on that.")
- The timeline vs. what happened: "A lot of times they're not" what
  actually happened. Between proposing problems and forming pods, "usually
  there's been a delay... Sometimes it's people aren't voting fast enough.
  So we wait."

The start of a Cycle and voting
- "The first couple of weeks are kind of you're sitting around twiddling
  your thumbs. You don't really know what you're supposed to be doing."
  People "go to the kickoff, and then they're ready to go, but then they
  have to wait to join the pod." (Her caveat: "I haven't done this. So this
  is just from my perspective.")
- "Too much stop, start, stop, start": put a problem in, vote, "then you
  have to register again for a pod."
- "I personally think there's too much voting." Alternatives: "have people
  sign up for a project, and if not enough people sign up, then have a
  conversation... or the first 5 people to sign up are part of that pod...
  or you could organize it by, these are the function areas we need
  filled."
- A move to 8 weeks "might be a good thing... get rid of a little bit of
  the [twiddling] of the thumbs... but only if we're actually ready to go."
- On Cycle 3's single pod: "I don't know if it's forcing people to
  organize too quickly."

Names and concepts
- Pods and projects: "we went away from the pod, and we went towards the
  project. I think that was also confusing, because there were pods and
  there were projects." In Cycle 1, "we didn't call them pods. We were
  trying to move away from that word, because we were applying for other
  grants. We use the word pod there." Whether it's confusing now: "that's a
  question to ask people."
- Dorst: she wasn't familiar with the name ("I'm not familiar with the term
  that you just used"). On the "for the curious" line: "that's new. That
  was an example of something that got introduced." It was "not used...
  for the first two, it was introduced for the third one." And: "you gave
  one thing, and then you add another thing. ... Just make that how we do
  it."
- MVP and iteration: "we're all perfectionists... there's different
  definitions of a minimally viable product... introducing that concept,
  sometimes the iteration... I don't necessarily think we say that enough
  in the beginning." Even when told it doesn't have to be perfect, "people
  still want it to be perfect."

Support and structure in early Cycles
- In Cycle 1, no one was embedded with the pods: "there's no pod
  moderator. It's just up to the team."
- Mentors: "reaching out to your mentors is important... some groups did a
  better job at that than others."
- On The Labs as "a safe place to try new things. And if you're not a coder,
  that's okay... try it." Secondhand: "from the feedback I've heard, that
  might not be happening."

Workshops
- Timing and location: "the time of... and location of some of the workshops
  have been led to fewer people coming. There are a couple of workshops I went
  to that were really great. No one was there." She's "always amazed how
  generous the tech people are with their time and their knowledge."
- Tied to the Cycle: "the goal is to plan them at the right time, so that
  people are going through the cycle. It would be meaningful to them," and
  "more people will participate than just those people going through the
  cycle... that bigger community." On timing: "There's supposed to be, but
  it's not perfectly."
- Not recorded: in-person sessions depend on library staff and equipment
  ("sometimes it works to record stuff, and sometimes it doesn't"), and
  virtual ones aren't recorded either ("I don't think so"). An idea she'd
  heard: record the virtual ones but not the in-person ones, "because part
  of the goal of being in person is to develop that community," and
  hands-on sessions don't film well.
- Do people have enough coming in? "No, I think that we could do better."
  What she'd want: "Here's the objectives of the week. Here's the workshops
  that are available. This is how this could help you get to that
  objective."
- GitHub: "I've heard... the GitHub was overwhelming to people who were not
  tech people." Of the GitHub sessions she attended: "the people leading
  them knew it, but they weren't fabulous teachers." Asked whether more or
  less hands-on would help: "more hands-on... come get tutoring almost...
  or get online, and here are resources... even some bullets on what GitHub
  is, and how you can use it."
- Of the AI agent workshop's facilitator (raised by the interviewer): "a
  fantastic teacher." That session had people working on their own
  computers while he "walked around and helped them."

Purpose and tracks
- "As an organization, what's our goal? Because if we're just people who
  are afraid of technology and AI, jumping right into GitHub is probably
  too much... it almost comes down to tracks": "introduction, or more
  advanced."
- An introductory period, drawing on a national youth program she knows:
  "I do think we should have an introductory period, which... people get
  introduced to GitHub and these other key things that they need to use. If
  we're gonna go that route."

The Learning Log
- She raised it unprompted: "some people did it, some people didn't do it.
  ... we really do need people to do that."
- Its purposes: a personal record ("you could look back and see what
  you've learned"); the energy question, "to get a sense of how are people
  feeling... one of the goals of the Upskilling Lab is creating a
  community"; data "that we can use when we're applying for grants" (are
  people engaged, would they recommend it); and "Are you stuck... was
  supposed to be for the Poderators, so that the Poderators could know if
  they needed to come in and get involved."
- "Some of them, I don't know if, from the evaluation perspective, it gives
  me much," for example "how effective was the collaboration with your pod
  this week." "I do think that maybe there's too many questions here."
  After the interviewer suggested simplifying it: "five or six questions
  would be good, shorter."
- Another organizer "has a lot of thoughts on this... different goals," so
  "it might be worth having a conversation with him."

Who comes to The Labs
- "Cycle one, a lot of people have been laid off. Cycle two, a little bit
  more steady. Cycle 3... has had the most steadily employed people."
- "One thing that's been consistent is it veers heavily towards women."

On AI (her own view)
- AI "can really help with some more routine functions... But [it] can't make
  up for the human connection. Oh, I know somebody who does that, let me
  introduce you."

The pilot, as an organizer described it
- About 50 people, before The Labs as it is now, who "met in person for 2
  hours... once a week," which required being available every week. Work
  groups also met in person.
- How one pilot group worked, as an organizer described it: members coded
  different parts (front end, back end) while sitting together, tried
  different tools, then agreed on one language and "came back together."
  People with no coding experience got one of the problems working, with
  help from someone who had it. She credited the time together: "the thing
  that made that work, kind of, is [being] together for two hours." She
  also said the way the work was split "maybe... [was] a group decision of
  our group."

My read so far (Nov 10):
- The in-person collaboration in the pilot surprised me. As an organizer
  described it, meeting in person for two hours every week was a required
  part of the pilot, and she credited that time for how the work got
  done. How the group split the work may have been the group's own choice
  ("maybe... a group decision of our group"). So the structure was the
  in-person time, not the way the work was divided.
- Follow-up (Nov 24): her understanding that "Are you stuck" was
  "supposed to be for the Poderators" didn't match how OLOS was set up.
  Poderators had no access to the Learning Log in OLOS, confirmed by an
  organizer or developer [check: role and date], and a Cycle 3 Poderator
  never saw it (#39). So the gap is a finding: the Learning Log's stated
  purpose for Poderators wasn't built into OLOS.
