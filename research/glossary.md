# Glossary: the words The Labs uses

A shared vocabulary for Build Cycles, built from evidence. Each entry records how a term is **written** in Labs materials, where to find evidence of how it's **actually used**, a **plain-language** version, and its **related practices** among recognized principles and practices (design thinking, human-centered design, continuous product development, rapid prototyping, lean startup, agile), using the same terms as the crosswalk in #20.

This is research, not a decision. It describes the vocabulary as it is today so the pods can agree on what it should be. The content model (metadata for each piece of learning) is a separate prototype.

**How to add to it**
- Quote the source for "As written." If there's no written definition, say so; that's a finding.
- "In practice" is a one-line pointer to the evidence: an interview (by role), a record, or the confusion log, by issue number. Log the evidence there first; the glossary holds definitions and the crosswalk, not the evidence itself.
- Leave a field as `[open]` rather than guessing.
- No names. This repo is public.

## Method (Dorst's frame creation)

A crosswalk to recognized principles and practices is in the Record "Connecting Dorst's frame creation to human-centered design and related practices" (#20, `research/record-dorst-and-related-practices.md`), checked by a maintainer against Dorst & Kokotovich (2015), the Double Diamond and the d.school modes. Use it when filling in the "Related practices" fields below.

### Frame creation
- **As written:** "Hard problems stay hard because everyone looks at them the same way... find the paradox... look for a frame" (C04 README).
- **In practice:** Not always named for participants: see #17 and #20.
- **Plain language:** [open]
- **Related practices:** [open]

### Archaeology
- **As written:** "Analyzing the history of the problem owner and of the initial problem formulation" (Dorst, quoted in the Scaffolding README). Dorst, *Frame Innovation* (2015), p. 74: "The first step of the frame creation process is to investigate in depth the apparent problem, as well as earlier attempts to solve it." It also calls for "an analysis of the hard, nonnegotiable boundaries ('They will never …')."
- **In practice:** See #20 and #23.
- **Plain language:** [open]
- **Related practices:** [open]

### Paradox
- **As written:** "two things that are both true and pull against each other" (C04 README). Civics & Elections formula: the value, the failure, the trap. Dorst's step, quoted in the Scaffolding README: "Analyzing the problem situation: what makes this hard?" The README adds: "Written as *because* statements... Then **set aside**. Frames come from the themes, not from staring at the paradox." Dorst, *Frame Innovation* (2015): "The lead question is: What makes this problem hard to solve?" (p. 74); "identify the core paradox or deadlock that keeps the problem owner from moving forward... express the paradox as a clash of rationalities in a series of 'because' statements" (p. 76); and "real-world paradoxes that are caused by conflicting values and needs on the problem side, or by the incommensurability of design outcomes on the solution side" (p. 52). His definition: "at the core of really 'hard' problems is a paradox. The word 'paradox' is used here rather loosely, in the sense of a complex statement that consists of two or more conflicting statements... All the statements that make up the paradox are (possibly) true or valid in their own right, but they cannot be combined for logical or pragmatic reasons" (pp. 50–51).
- **In practice:** [open]
- **Plain language:** [open]
- **Related practices:** [open]

### Context / Field
- **As written:** Context: "the inner circle: the players at The Labs who affect this problem today." Field: "everyone around the problem who isn't in the room" (Scaffolding README). Dorst's steps, quoted in the Scaffolding README: "Analyzing the inner circle of stakeholders" and "Exploring the broader societal field." Dorst, *Frame Innovation* (2015), p. 76: context is "an exploration of the practices of the inner circle of key stakeholders who have been involved in the problem situation before, or those who are clearly going to be necessary participants in any possible solution"; for field, "we... begin to radically widen the context, creating an intellectual, cultural, and social space we will here call a 'field' after Bourdieu."
- **In practice:** [open]
- **Plain language:** [open]
- **Related practices:** [open]

### Themes
- **As written:** "The deeper motivations that keep showing up across players... Themes are human, not technical" (Scaffolding README). Dorst's step, quoted in the Scaffolding README: "Investigating the themes that emerge in the broader field." Dorst, *Frame Innovation* (2015), p. 77: "we identify and seek to understand the deeper factors that underlie the needs, motivation, and experiences of the 'players' in this wider field." His definition: "Themes are a tool, a form of capturing the underlying phenomenon in a situation one tries to understand. Themes arise from the need or desire to comprehend—they are the sense we are able to make of a situation when we approach it openly, without prejudgment" (p. 66).
- **In practice:** [open]
- **Plain language:** [open]
- **Related practices:** [open]

### Frame
- **As written:** "a new way of seeing the problem that makes the paradox easier to move," written as "If we treat this problem as if it were a problem of [theme], then..." (READMEs). Dorst's step, quoted in the Scaffolding README: "Identifying patterns in the themes to create frames." Dorst, *Frame Innovation* (2015): "a frame is the proposal through which, by applying a particular pattern of relationships, we can create a desired outcome" (p. 53); "a frame is an organizational principle or a coherent set of statements that are useful to think with" (p. 63, his definition per the index); "If the problem situation is approached as if it is … , then …" (p. 78).
- **In practice:** [open]
- **Plain language:** [open]
- **Related practices:** [open]

### Futures / Transformation / Integration
- **As written:** Dorst's steps 7 to 9 (Scaffolding README): prototypes tested with three people; build and release weekly; "In use, someone keeps it, written up, taught back, pull request to OLOS." Dorst's steps, quoted in the Scaffolding README: "Exploring the possible outcomes and value propositions for the various stakeholders"; "Investigate the change in stakeholders' strategies and practices required for implementation"; "Draw lessons from the new approach and identify new opportunities within the network." Dorst, *Frame Innovation* (2015): in Futures, "a proposed frame is applied to the opened, broadened problem situation, it is then reshaped in a process of coevolution" (p. 78); Transformation is "a critical evaluation of what frames and solution directions would be feasible in the short term, or can be established gradually over a longer period of time" (p. 79); Integration is to "make sure that the new frames and the developments they initiate are well integrated into the broader context of the organizations involved" (p. 79).

## Related concepts (proposed, not yet in Labs materials)

These ideas come from outside The Labs. They're here because they may help
describe what The Labs is trying to do. None of them appear in Labs
materials yet, so each is marked as proposed.

### Community of practice
- **Source definition:** A group of people who share a concern or a
  craft and learn how to do it better by working on it together. Newcomers
  start at the edges and move toward the center as they gain skill
  ("legitimate peripheral participation"). (Lave & Wenger, *Situated
  Learning*, 1991; Wenger, *Communities of Practice*, 1998.)
- **Connection to The Labs (Claude draft, not yet reviewed):** The pod could be understood as a
  larger, cross-functional community of practice, not a team. That
  matches what the pod actually is before projects form, and it might
  ease the surprise a past participant described when pods split into
  smaller project teams ("you are too many here"). The contributor to
  maintainer ladder also resembles moving from the edge to the center.
- **Status:** Proposed. Also a possible frame ("If we treat the pod as a
  community of practice...").

### Self-organizing teams
- **Source definition:** Teams that decide for themselves how to do their
  work, rather than having tasks assigned. From the Agile Manifesto's
  principles: "The best architectures, requirements, and designs emerge
  from self-organizing teams" (2001); central to Scrum.
- **Connection to The Labs (Claude draft, not yet reviewed):** Project teams already form around
  a builder in residence and recruit their own members. Naming this would
  connect the Build Cycle to agile practice and to the Poderator's role as
  "not project managing."
- **Status:** Proposed.

### Self-Determination Theory (SDT)
- **Source definition:** A theory of motivation holding that people are
  most motivated when three needs are met: autonomy (choosing what you
  do), competence (feeling able to do it well) and relatedness (feeling
  connected to others). (Deci & Ryan.)
- **Connection to The Labs (Claude draft, not yet reviewed):** It maps closely onto the pod's
  question. Autonomy is the "learn by doing" side; competence is what
  scaffolding builds; relatedness is the pod and peer support. A past
  participant's most valuable learning was self-directed ("I just decided
  like, okay, you know what, this is what I want to learn"), while unclear
  structure left people unsure where to put their effort.
- **Status:** Proposed. Could serve as a lens for the paradox.

### Interoperable modules
- **Definition (proposed):** Modules built to a shared structure and
  vocabulary, so the parts fit together across people, pods and regions,
  and can be reused, combined and improved instead of rebuilt.
- **Connection to The Labs (Claude draft, not yet reviewed):** Ties the content model and taxonomy to
  Front Door's sketch (modules as Markdown in Git, an MCP server) and to
  the OLOS model: "The region builds and proves. The core keeps and
  carries" (C04 README). The Carpentries is an outside example: lessons
  share one template, so volunteers anywhere can teach and maintain them.
- **Status:** Proposed. Depends on agreeing a content model with Front
  Door.

### Kinds of principle and practice
The principles and practices people name together are different kinds of
thing. Sorting them makes the relationships clearer.

| Kind | What it is | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| **Principle** | An overarching way of working, with no steps of its own | Human-centered design |
| **Process** | A sequence of stages for working through a problem | Design thinking (IDEO's phases: Frame a question, Gather inspiration, Generate ideas, Make ideas tangible, Test to learn, Share the story; or the d.school modes: Empathize, Define, Ideate, Prototype, Test); the Double Diamond (Discover, Define, Develop, Deliver); Dorst's frame creation |
| **Method / tool** | A technique used at a particular stage, shared across processes | Rapid prototyping, user research and interviews, stakeholder mapping, affinity mapping, "How might we" questions, user testing |
| **Complementary practice** | A separate practice that builds on the processes above, mostly from prototyping onward | Lean startup (build-measure-learn, MVP), agile (sprints, releases, retrospectives), continuous product development |
| **Cadence / format** | How the work is organized in time and in events, across any step | Sprints (including the Sensemaking Sprint), weekly releases, hackathons, group-day milestones, check-ins, retrospectives |

How the Build Cycle combines them: Dorst's frame creation supplies the
steps (what to work on), sprints and releases supply the rhythm (when, in
what time-boxes), and methods like rapid prototyping supply the techniques
(how), all guided by the principle of human-centered design.
