# Interview: C03 project team participant who joined mid-way, C04 participant, new to GitHub and AI

Filed as issue #23 (Nov 9, 2026).

1. What it is
A conversation to learn whether she understood the process, whether she
had resources when she got stuck, and what would have been useful.

2. Where it came from
A C04 participant in another pod who also took part in Cycle 3.

3. When
Nov 9, 2026 (about 1.5 hours over two recordings, by video).

4. What it says
(Quotes are verbatim from the conversation, lightly trimmed with "...",
with filler words such as "like" and "you know" removed where they don't
change the meaning.)

Getting started
- Joined part-way through Cycle 3, after missing its start: "I had July
  13th on my calendar... was going to see the projects from Cycle 2 and then
  see the launch of Cycle 3," then had to go out of town. She didn't realize
  she could still join. "I jumped on the tail end of the Cycle 3 projects."
- Part-time: "I guess I would have a Swiss cheese approach because of my
  schedule." About an hour each way into DC. Has never attended a
  sense-making day or "an all-day sort of brainstorming."
- How she figured out what to do: "initially, I looked at the... cycle
  timeline graph to figure out where we were," then learned "in
  conversation, once I joined the project." "So timelines and deadlines, but
  you're kind of left on your own to figure out what your product is."
- Her Cycle 3 project's problem came from another participant, who "had the
  tech experience."

When she wasn't sure what to do
- "I did more research... it was more like asking the group what we can
  help with." Sometimes limited to an iPad. "Somebody volunteered to make
  the slides, and then the rest of us edited and provided feedback."
- "I can't just jump in myself and start coding... I don't want to mess and
  break anything. So I'm happy to write up stuff and do some more research."

Tools
- GitHub is new to her. She can read, post an issue and comment. On pull
  requests: "I don't know what that is or what it does or when I'm supposed
  to use it."
- The README's Claude links: "those links don't work for me... I have no
  clue of what the instructions that say, you do the thinking, let
  Claude do the administrative work... I don't know what that means."
- "I managed to go through my cycle contributing a little bit without
  having to learn the GitHub or sign up for Claude yet. But I feel like I
  could have learned more if I did."
- When another participant shared his screen: "Okay now I'm not up to speed
  anymore. What's happening?"

Workshops
- Attended in person: a storytelling-for-your-career workshop; a design
  thinking workshop after her Cycle: "that one might have been useful before
  the cycle or project piece." A GitHub workshop: "I don't think the
  timing's worked out." She knew a GitHub workshop was offered but couldn't
  attend (per the interviewer); two ran during her Cycle (Aug 18 and Sep

- "I've seen other people just say they're not quite sure how the workshops
  fit in with the cycle" (secondhand).
- Doesn't feel intimidated by workshops. For participatory ones, a
  read-ahead would help: "I'm not a good on-the-spot person."

Sense-making and C04 materials
- "Sometimes I'm not clear what comes out of the sense-making... what were
  the key takeaways and what were the key next steps."
- "For this cycle, they keep claiming that... quasi-problem statements were

- The pod READMEs: "they repeat across each scaffolding wayfinding... the

  somewhere in there you can find the question." "I'm expecting to find
  more, but then I go to the same sort of outline of where we are."
- Not familiar with Dorst: "some of the language is weird, like doing this
  archaeology phase or something, but I appreciate kind of what it means."
  Has a project management certification focused on agile.
- On Saturday's deliverable: "knowing what... you're going to pitch on
  Saturday is still kind of confusing." "I'm like, what is shipping?"

Structure

- Examples help: "it would be nice to go back and see the presentations

- Liked the issue templates: "there was a frame to post your information

The Learning Log and dashboard
- "I don't know who's looking at it... if you did it on your own, it was
  helpful for that individual." "My log needs attention, but I don't know if

- The dashboard's commitments box still showed Cycle 3 dates; "my feed is

Motivation

- Compliment (weak evidence): "most of what you've done is pretty good and

5. What it tells us (my read)

What's new: AI and the technology tools themselves. The first interviewee
already knew GitHub and the AI tools; this participant didn't. She can read
GitHub, post an issue and comment, but doesn't know what a pull request is,
and the Claude links in the README didn't work for her. She was also very
concerned about privacy, as was her prior organization ("I worry about data
security"; her employer didn't want "their information going into
algorithms or things that could get leaked").

What it backs up: workshops at fixed times and places don't reach everyone.
Workshops are optional for Build Cycle participants: they aren't among the
listed commitments, and an organizer has described them as complementary,
not required. She knew a GitHub workshop was offered but couldn't attend;
two ran during her Cycle (Aug 18, Sep 22). Both interviewees so far live
about an hour away in traffic and couldn't attend all workshops in person.
Like the first interviewee, she valued examples, self-learning and
flexibility.

The Labs already has a Learning Library, but outside workshops it holds
three guides (Slack Basics and the two "What to Expect" guides). What she
asked for isn't there: past examples ("see the presentations from the Cycle
2"), recordings ("it might even be archived, and I could... just watch the
recording"), read-aheads for participatory workshops, and a way to search
("there just needs to be a good search").

My recap to her near the end, as my read: "...some structure, guidepost,
and modular information would be helpful... maybe something before you do a
cycle, some resources. This is what I'm hearing from you." She agreed
("Yeah") and moved to timing: "we can do a trial next January... you
probably won't get it right, so you gotta plan for an evaluation."

Her own words earlier in the interview, before the recap, support it:
- Resources to self-learn from: "recordings or slides from some of the
  workshops that people could sort of self-learn from... There just needs
  to be a good search."
- Resources matched to where someone is: "some triggers that if... you're
  at a start of a cycle, here's relevant resources for a start of a cycle.
  Or... you're working on making a problem statement... somehow it has to
  be triggered when you know where the individual's at." And: "If there's a
  way to know where people are at."
- Defined stages, and prompts for people arriving different ways: "maybe
  if we have well-defined stages, and... they can say, hey, I'm new to the
  labs, I saw this workshop, it seemed interesting, what else can I get
  involved in? Or somebody told me to join this next cycle, and I want to
  start with... so you have to have enough of... somehow when
  the person enters, they have to have some prompts." Her examples: someone
  who came in through a single workshop and wants more, and someone told to
  join the next Cycle who doesn't know where to begin.
- Orientation: it "might have... directed me better to... what the labs
  thinks is the best orientation materials a little quicker."
- Examples and templates: "knowing how to find the past examples would be
  useful"; "if you had a template, what's your problem
  statement? Who's your target audience? Why?"
- Clear tasks: "a clear task, and having a deadline"; "some structure is
  good."
- Not too much at once: "this challenge of being too siloed... but then if
  I'm connected to too many things, I'm just gonna get overwhelmed."

Note on the GitHub finding in the first interview (#16): "without being
taught" refers to the pilot. GitHub workshops ran from May 2026.

What surprised me: I didn't realize she came in mid-Cycle. She had wanted
to join Cycle 2, but thought that because she missed the Summit and kickoff,
she had missed her chance. She then missed Cycle 3's kickoff too, but saw
she could still join a project team.

What she wants, in her words: "participating and learning more about the
process," "meeting new different people," "having an opportunity to sort of
engage in research," and, if the network grows, maybe "a Northern Virginia
group or something that'll be closer to me." On motivation: "if there's a
team goal and I could do something to help that succeed, that's motivating
for me."

6. Is any of this hearsay?
Mostly firsthand. Secondhand: that other people aren't sure how workshops
fit the Cycle ("I've seen other people just say..."), and that others in her
Cycle were in the same boat on time. Primed: she read the pod's recruiting
message (which shared the first interview's finding about levels and "start
from where you are") before the interview, was in the room at an evening
work session where modules were discussed (while working with another pod),
and said the modules-before-cycles idea came from "something in the GitHub
notes." What she said after the interviewer's recap and hypothesis near the
end is also weaker. "Knowing where people are at" was her phrase first; the
interviewer then echoed it. Compliment, weak evidence: "most of what you've done is
pretty good" (about the Labs). Quotes were checked against a transcript
whose speaker labels were corrected by the interviewer; the recording and
transcript stay off the repo.
