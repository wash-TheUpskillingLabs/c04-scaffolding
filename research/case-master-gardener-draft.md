## Who they are

A Cooperative Extension volunteer program. Adults get research-based
horticulture training from a land-grant university's Extension service,
then volunteer to answer public questions and run community programs. It
began in Washington State in 1973 and was in all 50 states by 1996.

## Sources to read, by section

### How it began (archaeology)

- In 1973, two Washington State University Extension agents, overwhelmed
  by public questions on garden pests, plant diseases and soil, trained
  volunteers to answer them. A trial clinic ran at a mall in 1972; 150
  people were accepted into the first class. (Search summary.)
- [WSU, "Seeds of a movement"](https://news.cahnrs.wsu.edu/article/seeds-of-a-movement-50-years-ago-master-gardeners-began-with-wsu-trained-volunteers)
- [WSU Magazine, "50 years, 50 states"](https://magazine.wsu.edu/2023/07/31/50-years-50-states/)
- [HistoryLink](https://www.historylink.org/File/8390)

### Why volunteers stay or leave (what happened)

- *Journal of Extension* (Dec 2013): satisfaction drives 61% of volunteers'
  intention to stay. [JOE 51(6)](https://open.clemson.edu/cgi/viewcontent.cgi?article=2499&context=joe)
- *Journal of Extension* (2002): retention rose with perceived personal
  benefits. [JOE 2002](https://archives.joe.org/joe/2002august/rb5.php)
- Mississippi, about 600 Master Gardeners: commitment and satisfaction best
  predicted intention to leave. [MSU thesis](https://scholarsjunction.msstate.edu/td/2177/)
- Programs "struggle with retention, particularly past four years."
  [UF project](https://aec.ifas.ufl.edu/media/aecifasufledu/formsdocs/non-thesis-projects/MGISTMasters.pdf)
- Some leave because they have no input into county activities.
  [Arkansas guide](https://www.uaex.uada.edu/yard-garden/master-gardeners/docs/recruitment_retention.pdf)
- Local association membership keeps people involved (five of eight county
  coordinators interviewed).
  [Purdue](https://ag.purdue.edu/department/extension/ppp/resources/ppp-publications/publications-archive/mobile/ppp-123/retaining-extension-master-gardener-volunteers-improving-communities-and-expanding-opportunities.html)
- Illinois study of inactive Master Gardeners: health and returning to
  full-time work cited, but no conclusive evidence why people left.
  [Illinois](https://www.ideals.illinois.edu/items/81720)
