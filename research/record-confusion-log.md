# Record: Things that confused a maintainer in C04

Filed as issue #17. Running list; add to it as you go.

1. What it is
A running list of places where C04 materials (or past Cycle materials) were
unclear, contradictory, or hard to find. Each entry: what it said, where, and
what turned out to be true. Add to it as you go.

2. Where it came from
A pod maintainer who was also a Civics & Elections participant.

3. When
Started Nov 6, 2026. Ongoing.

4. What it says
Grouped by kind. Each entry: what it said, where, and what turned out to be
true.

Contradictions between sources
- The dashboard shows the generic 12-week timeline with no dates or
  "you are here." C04 runs Oct 24 to Dec 8 (about 6.5 weeks) and was announced
  at Summit #3 as "an 8 week build cycle." Context: C04 started earlier, in
  early October, with a retreat (Oct 3), which may account for the
  difference (a maintainer's read).
- The dashboard listed "Internal Cycle" (starts Oct 13) as Upcoming and
  "Fall 2026" (Oct 13 to Dec 8) as Archived. Context: it appeared that
  participants were to sign up for the Cycle on the dashboard, but that
  seems to have been abandoned (a maintainer's observation).
- Two start dates for C04. The homepage says "From Oct 13 to Dec 8 we're
  running an internal Build Cycle"; the C04 README says Oct 24 to Dec 8.
  What turned out to be true: the README starts after pod formation; the
  homepage dates cover the whole Cycle (a maintainer). Neither says so.
- The Learning Log said she hadn't filled it out when she had. A C03
  participant and problem proposer (#42): "I was still getting things that
  said I hadn't filled out my learning log when I did," so she filled it
  out again and found "2 dates on there" for the same week. "It might have
  been me." Entries also erase if you leave before saving: "you have to
  save it all at one time." What turned out to be true: not yet known; a
  likely bug [check: whoever maintains the form in OLOS].
- Who reads the Learning Log. On the dashboard, it goes to "your
  Poderator" (see the glossary). An organizer understood it was "supposed
  to be for the Poderators" (#28). A Cycle 3 Poderator never saw it, and
  Poderators had no access in OLOS (#39). A C03 participant and problem
  proposer (#42) reported being stuck in it ("I did, I did, I did") and
  no one reached out. What turned out to be true: Poderators couldn't see
  it (#39). Who, if anyone, did read it is still open.

Hard to find
- In Civics & Elections, the assignments and week-by-week schedule lived in
  two Google Docs linked from the public homepage, theupskillinglabs.org, not
  on the dashboard's main view. A participant who finished the Cycle never found them.
  Later phases were "Exact date TBD." Where they are: the homepage's
  "Learning Library · on-demand" section ("Learn at your own pace") lists
  three guides: "Slack Basics," "What to Expect in a Build Cycle" ("A
  quick-start guide to joining the build cycle, including key dates") and
  "What to Expect: From Pod to Hackathon" ("An overview of the two week
  sprint from pod formation to the Hackathon on August 15th"). Logged out,
  the section is on the homepage, below the fold. Logged in, the homepage
  is the dashboard. Its Library tab, next to Home, opens on Luma workshop
  events; the three self-learning guides are below the fold on that page.
  There's also a link to the Library in the lower left of the dashboard,
  below the fold (viewed Nov 9). Those three guides are the only
  self-learning material there. A maintainer's read: easy to miss, which
  explains why a participant missed them.
- C04 deliverables (e.g., "What surprised us," due Nov 2) appear only in the
  pod README table. Not on the dashboard, no notification. The assignment
  itself is defined in one line: "The pod posts one issue titled 'What
  surprised us': three things."
- C04 runs entirely on GitHub, not the dashboard, though the dashboard
  lists "Internal Cycle" as Upcoming (there may once have been a plan to
  use it; a maintainer's guess). A maintainer's view: for an internal Cycle
  this is fine and carries little weight. For an external Cycle, the same
  split would be far more confusing.
- What a Cycle looks like, before you join. A C03 participant and
  problem proposer (#42) came in through a library event and searched
  online for what The Labs was, finding a recorded roundtable: "I'm
  looking at the flyers, I'm reading the stuff... what does that look
  like?" She wanted examples of past work and of what a cohort does: "a
  reference of what you've already done. 'Cause I was looking for that."
- What the hackathon would be. The same participant (#42): "I didn't know
  what to expect at the hackathon... I just know that it was all day."
  Each presenter's topic wasn't given up front, so she couldn't tell what
  was relevant to what she was building.
- Where to go after a session. The same participant (#42): "once you go
  to the hackathon, that's your only time interfacing with that, and then
  after that, you have to figure out where to go to learn about this." And
  for anyone new: "if I want to know more, where do I go?"
- Luma. The same participant (#42) was told about Slack, OLOS and Luma at
  the start, but "I don't think everyone probably knew to [go on] Luma."

Undefined terms
- The role of design thinking at The Labs. At the Aug 15 hackathon, design
  thinking was offered as morning workshops on the Newcomer track,
  described as catching up for newcomers (Luma:
  https://luma.com/theupskillinglabs?period=past). Those already signed up
  for the Build Cycle were on the build track, starting individual
  prototypes while the workshops ran. Open question: what role does design
  thinking play at The Labs? Is it treated as an overarching principle,
  like human-centered design?
  From the workshop room: a C03 participant and problem proposer (#42) was
  in the room with the workshops, including a problem-solving workshop she
  still applies "to most of the things that I do." The maintainer was on
  the build track and missed it. Her view: "If we're doing this all as a
  whole collective, we need to all be able to experience those."
- The Build Cycle is built on Dorst's frame creation and works in sprints,
  but participants weren't always told the method's name (it was linked
  "for the curious"). Its terms (archaeology, paradox, context, field,
  themes, frames) aren't yet connected to recognized principles and
  practices, which is an opportunity (see the Record "Connecting Dorst's
  frame creation to human-centered design and related practices," #20).
- "The judgment the Cycle is for." The Scaffolding pod's page and the C04
  invite copy say "People handed a syllabus never build the judgment the
  Cycle is for." A past participant who did two Cycles wrote: "I still
  don't know what exactly is 'the judgment the Cycle is for'?" (#16). Looking
  for how The Labs defines judgment, a maintainer found it in "What to
  Expect: The Build Cycle": "We think the judgement of what problems matter
  remains uniquely human."
- "Syllabus," in the same line, can mean a schedule of what and when, or
  step-by-step instructions on how; the copy doesn't say which.
- "Scaffolding," the pod's own name, isn't defined anywhere. In the
  Scaffolding README, the C04 README and the pod's page, it appears only as
  the pod's name, never as a concept. While drafting a finding, the maintainer used the word three different
  ways in one paragraph:
  1. The education term. In learning research, "scaffolding" (Wood, Bruner
     & Ross, 1976) means temporary support that helps a learner do
     something just beyond what they can do alone, and that's removed as
     they get better at it. The defining feature is that it comes down.
  2. A synonym for structure. "Scaffolding only works when participants
     can see what it's for" used it loosely, to mean the Cycle's structure
     in general.
  3. One item in a list. "The scaffolding, terminology, and support feel
     misaligned" treated it as separate from support, even though in sense
     1 support is scaffolding. So the list counted the same thing twice.
- How much support there is, by design. A C03 participant and problem
  proposer (#42): "I was kind of told once I got in the group that... it
  was kind of made to have you in a real world scenario to... figure out
  how to fix these problems with a group with not... maybe not much
  support." Who told her isn't named. Asking her Poderator when stuck,
  "the thing that I was stuck about, I feel like there wasn't support
  around it." What turned out to be true: not yet known; compare the
  Poderators' direction to hold back (#29, #39).
- Tool discounts. The same participant (#42) understood, from a mentor,
  that The Labs doesn't get educational discounts the way schools do, so
  Lovable credits came through mentors. She also asked whether The Labs
  can't work with teenagers because of age limits, from something an
  organizer said. The interviewer didn't know. [check: an organizer]

A label that doesn't match practice
- "Group days," as listed in the README ("when the whole Cycle meets"),
  sound like meetings, but they're asynchronous milestones (learned at an
  in-person session, Nov 5). It's unclear whether there are other group
  work days besides the weekly coworking meetings.
- "Pod" vs. "project team." As a past participant, a maintainer found the
  two easy to confuse; at a former workplace, pods came together as
  project teams. A past participant interviewed on Nov 7 (#16) saw the same
  thing: not everyone understood that pods would be broken down. People
  started working as if the pod were their team, then "you realize, oh,
  you are too many here. You definitely are not going to be on the same
  project. You have to do something different. And that took people
  aback." And: "you would be like working with some people and then you
  realize, oh, but I might not end up on the same project with... the
  group."
  A C03 participant and problem proposer (#42): "I knew that we were going
  to have [pods]... But I didn't know what that was gonna look like." She
  had already built her site at the hackathon, and others had their own
  projects: "Maybe the pod should be before the hackathon, so that the
  groups could be formed... I'm not sure how that was intended."
