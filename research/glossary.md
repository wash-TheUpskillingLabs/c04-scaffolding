# Glossary

Terms the Scaffolding pod uses, and where each one comes from. Add to it as terms come up.

## Scaffolding

In learning research (Wood, Bruner & Ross, 1976), temporary support that helps a learner do something just beyond what they can do alone, and that's removed as they get better at it. The defining feature is that it comes down.

Watch for two loose uses (see the confusion log, #17): as a synonym for the Cycle's structure in general, and as one item in a list next to "support," which counts the same thing twice.

## Where someone is

*Open: not yet defined.* Used in the pod's working scope: "matching structure to where someone is, across modules, workshops and the Cycle." The first interview (#16) suggests at least three meanings:

- **Skill level.** A workshop that assumed she knew what an agent was.
- **What they came for.** Direction and a way back to work, a startup idea already in hand, or a specific skill.
- **Stage of the Cycle.** Pod vs. project, and structure that came "too slow, then too fast."

## From whom

Who provides structure or support:

- **Volunteer SMEs and mentors.**
- **Others in the Build Cycle (peers),** in two ways:
  - *Peers she learned from:* small comments from teammates, like coding in layers and separating front end from back end ("I learned a few things here and there, even from the non-technical people"), and finding out that meetings can be transcribed (#16).
  - *Peers who filled gaps, unplanned:* she set up the GitHub repo and explained the process to others, and said "it wasn't my job" (#16).
- **Materials made available for self-directed learning.** Partial evidence: she wanted hands-on GitHub help; the background-material idea came from the interviewer (#16).

*Open: organizers and Poderators also appear in #16. Do they belong here?*

## GitHub

A website where projects are kept in repositories, with every change tracked. People propose changes through pull requests and discuss work in issues. At The Labs, each pod's research and project code live there, and organizers want to keep using it (from workshops with organizers). In #16, it was the tool her team was expected to use, and she was the one who knew it.
