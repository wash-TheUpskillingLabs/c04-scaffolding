# Case (draft): Pursuit, from coding fellowship to AI-Native cohorts

**Status: draft, chosen by the builder, not yet read.** Sources gathered
Nov 24, 2026, from search summaries. Almost all are Pursuit's own pages;
check every figure at the source. The builder's summary (what they tried,
what happened, why) is still to come.

Chosen because it is an adult program running build cohorts in the AI era,
and it rebuilt its structure around AI in 2025. Its participants are called
"Builders."

## Who they are

A New York City nonprofit (Long Island City, Queens) that trains low-income
adults for tech jobs. Founded in 2011 as Coalition for Queens (C4Q);
renamed Pursuit in 2018. (Dates differ slightly across sources.)

## What they tried (sources to read)

The AI-Native program as Pursuit describes it (self-reported):

- **Stages:** AI Literacy, AI Build, Get Hired; or "three progressive
  levels, each raising the complexity, autonomy, and real-world stakes."
- **From whom:** "a unique many to many learning model powered by peer
  teaching"; "not one expert to many students, but builders at every stage
  teaching and learning from each other." Cross-stage teams with staff,
  alumni and guests.
- **Supports:** a personalized daily plan, an AI Coach for instant
  feedback, adaptive coursework; mini-hackathons.

### The launch email: "AI-native pilot launch—a new 0 to 1 moment for Pursuit" (posted Mar 25, 2025)

[Source](https://www.pursuit.org/blog/ai-native-pilot-launch). An internal
email from Pursuit's CEO to its boards and staff, Mar 18, 2025, later
posted publicly. Text pasted by the builder. People by role.

**Structure, before and after**
- "Shifting our 'human-instructor'-to-participants ratio from 1:18 to
  1:100. We are able to supplement 'human-instructor' with 'AI-instructor'
  on the new Pursuit training platform."
- "The pilot itself is streamlined to seven months, with modular two-month
  off-ramps, significantly shorter than our traditional 12–18-month
  programs."
- "Builders are taught by human-instructors, AI-instructors (via LLM and
  our platform), as well as by volunteers and their peers." A separate
  platform captures feedback from volunteers.
- "'Learn to learn' is the first milestone of the pilot."
- An "initial two-month nights and weekend module." The CEO joined it,
  aiming "to meet the pilot standard of 85% persistence/attendance."
- Builders were required "to actively participate and engage on X as a
  way of learning as well as potentially leapfrogging the traditional
  hiring process."
- "We've intentionally adopted the term 'Builders' instead of 'Fellows.'
  Our Builders aren't just participants—they're actively shaping Pursuit's
  future by testing new software... providing direct feedback."
- Staff: from no org-wide software development (2024 and before), to
  monthly build sessions (Jan–Feb 2025), to weekly, "at least 1 day a week
  (20% of our time)."

**Who came** (first cohort, as reported)
- 104 Builders started Mar 15, 2025, from "over 1,200 interested
  applicants."
- "51% without college; 40% unemployed; 46% on gov't assistance and 9%
  public housing residents; $15K average income."
- Application cycle 2 weeks (was 6); recruitment cycle 6 weeks (was 16).

**Why, in the CEO's words**
- "Evolve or Die."
- "Faster, Cheaper, and (hopefully) Better." "We've accomplished this while
  reducing our overall expense budget by 38% compared to our December
  Board-approved plan."
- "Although it's early, we're hopeful that this will also have better
  results." "We won't get everything right... that's why we are running
  this pilot."
- "There are real, significant challenges facing job training
  organizations, the social sector, and Pursuit right now."
- A New York Times reporter was embedded with the pilot.

### Pursuit's own account: "Pursuit's AI Transformation: Products and Processes We Built in 2025" (Jan 26, 2026)

[Source](https://www.pursuit.org/blog/2025-builds). Text pasted by the
builder (the site is blocked in this session). Self-reported, no outcome
data.

**Why, in Pursuit's words**
- "Pursuit entered 2025 with one guiding principle: evolve or die. We knew
  that to continue to generate life-changing outcomes, we'd have to rebuild
  from the ground up to be AI-Native... it meant transforming Pursuit
  itself."
- From Jan 15, 2025, expectations for staff: "We rethink priors. We all
  build software. We move quickly and iterate."
- Twelve months later, by Pursuit's account: "we've cut our SaaS spend in
  half, built in-house workforce development software that evolves with
  the job market, and collected data that helps us operate better and land
  more jobs for the community we serve." (No figures given for jobs.)

**Build Days: how staff learned** (same page)
- "Once a week, staff from all departments would clear their calendars to
  build with AI." Headline: "Carving out 20% of staff time to learn,
  experiment, and Build."
- "Reception was mixed—some worried it would derail urgent work. Others
  doubted AI's value."
- "What started with personal projects... eventually became structured and
  strategic." Then, in order: early prototypes (a self check-in app for
  Builder attendance, a volunteer management tool); "an intake process to
  channel builds toward organizational priorities"; "We started building in
  teams. We all learned what a good PRD looks like."
- "We are dogfooding our own mission: teaching ourselves to build with AI
  the same way we teach our Builders."
- "By year's end, we'd effectively added 14 contributing engineers to our
  team—not by hiring, but by upskilling the staff already here." "Now, 78%
  of non-technical staff have contributed code to our production-level
  apps." (Self-reported; how "contributed code" is counted is not given.)
- Pursuit's lessons: "It has to be fun before it's productive." "Cultural
  transformation is harder than skill development." "Experimentation dies
  without protected time & permission to fail."
- Pilots of Build Days with partner organizations (Neighborhood Trust,
  MetLife): "Participants entered with minimal AI knowledge and left with
  real products they had built themselves." (No figures.)

**The Admissions Portal: first staff-built product** (same page)
- "When our admissions software came up for renewal, we made our first
  org-wide bet: could we replace it with a 'vibe-coded' product built by
  non-technical staff?" "We put together a team, defined requirements, and
  got to building. Four weeks later, we shipped."
- Features listed: an agent that "evaluates incoming applications and
  recommends next steps"; automated applicant email; in-app onboarding.
- "Were there bugs and glitches at launch? Absolutely." By Pursuit's
  account: three SaaS licenses canceled, faster recruitment, "four cohorts
  of high-potential learners in just ten months." (No figures for speed.)
- Pursuit's lessons: "Merge conflicts teach collaboration faster than any
  workshop." "Collaborative building taught us what alignment actually
  means—not nodding at requirements, but genuinely understanding who needs
  what and why."

**The Data Layer** (same page)
- "Our coaches have started to think like product managers... So they built
  a data layer themselves."
- AI Coach conversation history is captured to diagnose "the exact point of
  confusion." A real-time dashboard shows staff "who needs help and who's
  ready to help others—then act on it immediately." Aggregated
  conversation data "dictates program evolution."
- Figures on a graphic on the page (activity, not outcomes; period not
  stated): 511K messages across 30K learning conversations (25M words);
  9,600 Builder assignment submissions analyzed with feedback; 431 Builder
  demo videos assessed by AI; 4,079 peer-to-peer feedback interactions
  analyzed; "8x growth in task analysis capability over 6 months"; 398
  curriculum changes "driven by data insights."
- Pursuit's lessons: "The best learning data captures process, not just
  outcomes." "The hardest part wasn't building the infrastructure, it was
  changing how decisions get made—moving from intuition to evidence
  required deliberate practice."

**Connecting Builders to jobs** (same page)
- Tools: Pathfinder (Builders log projects and contacts aligned with
  target roles), Lookbook (public directory of Builders' skills and
  projects for hiring partners), Sputnik (staff CRM for employer outreach).
- Pursuit's lessons: "The market is shifting too quickly to train for
  specific roles—the best thing we can teach is how to 'learn to learn' and
  adapt quickly." "Employers are still figuring out what they need from
  AI-native talent."
- **On outcomes, in Pursuit's own words:** "Our pilot cohort has just
  entered the job search phase of our program. 2026 will tell us whether
  our data compounds into genuine market intelligence or just stays noise."
  (As of Jan 26, 2026.)

**Looking ahead** (same page, Jan 26, 2026; written by a Pursuit staff
member)
- "Last year proved we could transform how we operate. This year we're
  focusing on jobs, and achieving product-market fit for our Builders."
- "A lot is still uncertain."

**Small business AI Demo Day with Hispanic Federation** (Pursuit post,
Oct 7, 2026, by a Pursuit general manager; text pasted by the builder;
people described by role)
- Ten Builders were each paired with a small business identified through
  Hispanic Federation, with support from Google, Amazon, Jobs for the
  Future and the New York City Council. Demo Day: Sep 23, 2026. Builds
  included a financial dashboard for a painting contractor, a dispatching
  tool for a maintenance company, and bilingual intake and communication
  tools.
- How it was structured, in Pursuit's words: "Each pairing was chosen
  intentionally, connecting the business's needs with the Builder's skills
  and experience." "Over several weeks, Builders and owners spent time
  together, visited workplaces, and learned from each other." "Across the
  presentations, it was evident that understanding the business came
  first."
- Who the Builders were: people with backgrounds "in the trades,
  education, healthcare, and the arts"; one had worked 15 years as a cable
  technician. "Many of whom entered the program without previous
  experience in technology."
- Trust came through the community partner: an owner said an
  introductory workshop with Hispanic Federation and direct conversations
  with Pursuit "helped him trust the process."
- Results reported: "working products and owners' early assessments"
  (one owner: "nothing short of transformative"). A city council member
  called for "support to track whether the tools help businesses grow
  stronger over time." The post: "Continued support would allow partners to
  measure sustained use, time saved, business growth, and career outcomes
  for Builders." **No career outcome figures for Builders.**
- Businesses may engage their Builders for further paid projects or hire
  them.

**The AI Jobs Institute** (Pursuit post, Oct 22, 2026; text pasted by the
builder; [Governor's announcement](https://www.governor.ny.gov/news/governor-hochul-announces-launch-ai-jobs-institute-prepare-new-yorkers-jobs-future))
- New York's Governor announced a training hub in Long Island City that
  Pursuit will build and run as its new headquarters. Goal: "serve up to
  40,000 New Yorkers by 2032." Construction starts winter 2026–27; move-in
  expected mid-2027.
- Funding: "$10 million of public investment," including $3.06 million
  from Empire State Development, plus a coalition of philanthropic and
  industry partners (among them Google.org, OpenAI, Anthropic, Wells Fargo,
  MetLife Foundation, Ascendium, Siegel Family Endowment).
- Four areas: workforce training, innovation pilots (including with small
  businesses and nonprofits), policy and research, and convening
  employers.
- A National Advisory Council chaired by a former US Secretary of
  Education, with leaders from foundations, AI companies, CUNY, workforce
  nonprofits and state government.
- Why, in Pursuit's words: "AI is creating new kinds of work faster than
  our training systems were built to handle. Traditional programs can take
  years to design and launch." The gap "hits hardest for the New Yorkers
  with the least room for error: people without a degree or a network in
  tech, who cannot afford to take a year off to retrain."
- On results: "The model works, and it has produced some of the strongest
  earnings gains in our field." This refers to Pursuit's decade of work;
  **the post gives no outcome figures for the AI-Native cohorts.**

**The change**
- "We scrapped our traditional software development curriculum and shifted
  to a new model focused on teaching our community how to build software
  with AI."
- "To learn AI, you have to use AI. Our builders have each spent ~1,000
  hours building with AI throughout the program—not learning about AI
  through lectures, but developing the muscle memory and intuition that
  comes from daily practice."

**Who built the platform**
- "Our team built the training platform themselves. The same people who'd
  spent years in our classrooms—understanding exactly where learners get
  stuck, what tone resonates, what sequencing works—could now encode those
  decades of institutional knowledge directly into software."
- The Builder platform: an **AI Coach** that "delivers content and
  amplifies the in-person learning experience"; **curriculum generation**
  to "stay flexible with the job market"; **feedback agents** that "track
  progress... with personalized guidance."
- Builders and staff use multiple AI models (via OpenRouter and Claude).
- Pursuit uses AI to evaluate whether its AI Coach's responses are
  "helpful, appropriately toned, and pedagogically sound."

**Pursuit's stated lessons** (two lists on the page)
- "When the people closest to the problem can build the solution,
  iteration cycles collapse."
- "1,000 hours of building with AI creates superusers."
- "AI doesn't replace human instruction—it amplifies it by handling content
  delivery so teachers focus on community building, teamwork, and the human
  interventions that drive outcomes."
- "Experimentation requires ruthlessly protected time - without it, daily
  urgency always wins."
- "When domain experts become builders, everything accelerates."
- "The technology is easy, the transformation is hard."

## Pursuit's hiring and funder pages (text pasted by the builder, Nov 24, 2026)

[Hire page](https://www.pursuit.org/hire), [funders page](https://www.pursuit.org/our-funders).
Pursuit's own claims, written for employers and funders.

**Program structure, as stated**
- "Seven months. More than 630 hours. Fully in person." Builders "build AI
  products for real organizations from the first weeks."
- **Discrepancy to check:** this says 630+ hours; the January 2026 review
  says Builders spend "~1,000 hours building with AI." Possibly program
  hours vs. total hours including practice; not explained.
- Employers "shape our curriculum, sponsor Builder projects, and work with
  Builders through paid deployments and apprenticeships before hiring."
- "We stay with Builders through their first years on the job, because
  getting hired and staying hired are different challenges."

**Outcome claims** (none says which program or years; most likely the
earlier Pursuit Core fellowship, since AI-Native began in 2025)
- "92% are still employed three years after placement, compared with a
  64% industry benchmark, and 97% of eligible Builders have earned a
  promotion." Benchmark source not given.
- "Builders enter earning an average of $18,000 and start their careers at
  more than $94,000." "Since 2013... an estimated $1 billion in lifetime
  wage gains."
- Citi: 37 Pursuit graduates hired after Citi removed a degree requirement
  for a pathway into its Summer Analyst program. Union Square Ventures
  portfolio: 13 engineers placed, rated 8.2/10 by managers, 100% retention.
  Dates not given.
- A startup CEO's testimonial about a Builder.

**Three ways employers hire:** an open role (matched candidates, two to
four weeks, no placement fees); co-designing a role around a problem; or
starting with a scoped AI project before hiring.

**Beyond the cohort**
- Free AI literacy workshops in English and Spanish with NYCHA, the New
  York and Queens public libraries and community groups: "about 2,000
  people in 2025," "on track for more than 5,000 in 2026."
- "AI Build Corps": Builders embedded in nonprofits and small businesses
  as paid "forward deployed engineers."
- New York's "first Registered Apprenticeship in Software Engineering";
  named the State's partner for "AI Prep for Jobs."
- Its curriculum now runs on its platform at other organizations,
  including Per Scholas in Buffalo and Le Moyne College in Syracuse (see
  Per Scholas in [case leads](case-leads.md)).
