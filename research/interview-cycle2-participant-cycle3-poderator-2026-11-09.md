# Interview: Cycle 2 participant and Cycle 3 Poderator on pod-to-team changes, getting set up on GitHub, and when a Poderator should step in

Filed as issue #39 (Nov 24, 2026).

1. What it is
A conversation about structure from both sides: his experience as a
participant in Cycle 2, and what he saw and was allowed to do as a
Poderator in Cycle 3.

2. Where it came from
A past participant (Cycle 2) who was a Poderator in Cycle 3. Product
background; no coding experience before the Labs. In Cycle 2 his problem
statement became one of the pod's projects.

How he relates to earlier interviews: he was in the same Cycle 2 pod as
the other Poderator recruited by the interviewee in #29, and his Cycle 2
Poderator was the interviewee in #29. He was a Poderator in the Cycle the
maintainer took part in (#26, #30).

3. When
Nov 9, 2026 (about 70 minutes, by video), the same day as #28. Speaker
labels in the transcript were checked; the meeting tool's two summaries
listed several of the interviewer's suggestions as "agreements" and were
not used as a source. The interviewer's raw notes were checked against
the transcript.

4. What it says
(Quotes are verbatim from the transcript, lightly trimmed with "...", with

How he came in (Cycle 2)
- He audited first: "I had gone to some of the information sessions of
  Build Cycle 1... sort of had audited what the program was gonna be."
  Then: "I could use some upskilling in these areas... It's very project
  based." He submitted a problem statement, and it became one of the
  pod's projects.
- What he wanted: "to experience building something that was using some
  of the latest AI tools and get experience doing that, with a bunch of
  other people who are kind of in a similar situation." "Nobody in my team
  had any experience with coding, or building anything, so we were all
  kind of learning at the same time."
- "I wasn't expecting it to be very structured, to be fair, but I was
  expecting it to be more structured than it was."

The Hackathon (Cycle 2)
- "I did not expect to get as much out of it as I got out of it."
- Mentor sessions ran in one part of the building while teams built in
  the main area. Several teammates wanted to go to the sessions; their
  Poderator "kept saying, well, I think you actually would get more out of
  just staying here... so we just ultimately stayed. But I remember being
  quite confused about that... I felt like I missed out." He gave that
  feedback at the time.
- "We did most of the project work at the hackathon, just by accident."

From pod to project teams
- "They took the Pod, and then they broke the Pod up into... problem
  statement teams... They forced us to submit the problem statement...
  with a team of 5." He and another participant in the same pod asked to
  submit the same problem and "dovetail each other's work"; the organizers
  "tried to pull us apart" so the two teams took different approaches,
  "which is ultimately... a good thing."
- "They essentially broke up the band... That took some air out... because
  we just didn't really understand it, and they didn't explain it very
  well. But ultimately, we rallied."

After the Hackathon: unstructured
- "Things got really unstructured after the hackathon. We were meant to
  self-organize and we were self-organizing."
- "During that project phase, it was also highly unstructured... a lot of
  us just didn't know where to go next. We didn't really know what we were
  supposed to be doing. And we weren't given a lot of guidance."
- Did he know what was coming next, week to week? "No... that was the one
  feedback that resoundingly everybody in our cycle gave... we did not
  know what was coming next."

Communication, Cycle 2 vs. Cycle 3
- Cycle 2: "it was terrible... hardly anything was communicated. Everybody
  was confused as to what to do by when. There was not a lot of messaging
  about what to do, and the deliverables at different stages."
- Cycle 3: the two Upskiller Success Team co-leads (he calls them "our
  Poderator leads") "did a much better job... It was noticeably better."
  He told them so during the Cycle. (The emails and
  "What to Expect" guides in #24: "we had none of that.")

GitHub and getting set up (Cycle 2)
- A GitHub 101 session: "It was terrible. It was not a 101... It was not a
  GitHub for dummies." It assumed people "had already had experience with
  it, and had GitHub and Claude Code on their machines." Before it: "they
  were like, you can show up... just show up and learn." In the session,
  "we took almost the entire session on trying to get it going, and
  ultimately not understanding what we were doing... most of us walked out
  of there without it set up... a colossal failure."
- Getting there: the intro sessions have "been in Capitol Hill... It was in
  the middle of the day... I just took 2 or 3 hours out of my day to get
  here."
- Who taught it: early on he calls it the mentor's "GitHub 101"; later he
  says the 101 "wasn't [the mentor]. It was another woman," and the mentor
  ran "GitHub 102." His later, more detailed account is used here.
- How his team got set up: a teammate who joined at the Hackathon went to
  the mentor's 102 session, "asked for help," had a one-on-one, and "put
  together this cheat sheet in Google Docs that everybody could use... a
  step-by-step document on how to set up a project in GitHub and connect it
  into Claude Code." "We all did that kind of asynchronously, and that's
  how we started building the project."
- They worked as a team in one repository: "everybody was working from the
  same instance... had instances on their own machines... and then checked
  it into the main repository." The teammate fed the team's PRD into
  Claude, then assigned pages: "This is your page. Go do it."
- Other sessions: "That one session has been like the only one black point
  that I can think of... the vast majority of the other sessions were
  excellent."

What made it click
- "Honestly the chemistry amongst the other team members... we just had a
  good team." "If the team fits well, and everybody's self-motivated...
  then the thing goes well. If it's not that way, then it's harder."
- "Everybody knew that people were going to be busy and were going to work
  asynchronously... Other team members would help them, or we could get
  help from the labs."
- Team placement: "we were sort of placed randomly together." "It's hard
  to know... if the teams are really gonna fit... nobody really knew each
  other."
- Being in the same boat: "Everybody was pretty much all in the same boat
  ... we were all learning the same thing, and wanting to... get that
  experience." His guess at the alternative: "My experiences might have
  been different had I been put into a project where you had experienced
  coders who wanted to do a lot of that themselves." (A hypothetical, not
  something he saw.)
- Another team in the same pod, as its member told him: "out of five of
  us, it was really only two of us that were doing a lot of the stuff."
  (Hearsay; see section 6.)
- Presenting: teammates kept asking him to present because "it's your
  problem... you can tell the story better than anybody." He did two of
  three, then "for the big show, I kind of stepped back."

As a Poderator (Cycle 3)
- "I tried to help them multiple times, and I tried to insert myself... I
  was kind of told not really... the folks in the project need to take
  that assistance, or elect to get it." "I was discouraged... by the
  Upskilling Labs."
- What he wanted to be able to do: "At what point, if the pod is really
  struggling, do you... say, okay, I'm gonna set up a meeting... here's the
  next steps? That's what I thought I could do. But I was discouraged to do
  that... Everybody was like, well, it's meant to be self-organizing."
- His Cycle 2 Poderator, asked afterwards why they hadn't helped organize
  more: "well, we were discouraged." (Secondhand; see section 6.)
- What a Poderator could see: "I could see all the channels that we're
  running... You could tell which ones were better organized... and were
  doing fine without any intervention."
- The team he was assigned to as Poderator: one project's problem proposer,
  a coder by background, "started building stuff right away" before defining
  the problem; the team ran short of time and cut scope. (His account; an
  observation about another participant.) "I offered it several times, I
  was taken up on it a couple times." "I regret not being able to do that
  for them." How receptive the team would have been: "I don't honestly
  know."
- Did that team know what to do next? "No." Because they didn't engage
  with the materials? "A mixture of that," and the project's direction.
- Whether every Cycle has a team like this: "I think every cycle had
  probably had one of these."

Unstructured by design
- "I think that's by intent... there's this balance that I think the labs
  is trying to strike... They don't want it to be a step-by-step... They
  want it to be somewhat freeform. And that's okay... I think it works
  well, when you have self-motivating teams... But for those who need a
  little bit more guidance, I think the labs should probably step in."
- "I'm not advocating for the extreme case at all, because I like the fact
  that it was self-organizing, and people had to figure stuff out... not
  have it completely scripted to them, like, that's the best way to learn."
- At the close: "knowing when to put more of a hand on the rudder... at
  some point, you gotta steer the ship instead of leaving it just to
  flail around the waters by itself."

The weekly check-ins (Learning Log)
- In Cycle 2 they were "check-ins. They were like surveys... how are you
  doing this week? What do you need help with?" "If you didn't do those,
  then you lost access to... I don't know, Slack, or I don't know,
  something." "So everybody kind of did those."
- "I always wondered what they were doing with that information."
- As a Poderator in Cycle 3: "I never saw any of that stuff either... If
  that was available to me... Nobody told me where it was." He assumed the
  Success Team co-leads had access: "But I don't know."

Self-guided learning
- "Particularly some of the getting started stuff... how to set up GitHub,
  and how to set up Claude... if you're given a cheat sheet, like
  [his teammate] did for our team... and if you had problems, you could
  then reach out to [the mentor]... I think that would be helpful."
- "For me, I like to learn by doing. And that is the fundamental thing that
  makes Upskilling Labs so cool."
- "Here's some prerequisites... things that you should become familiar
  with, or already... have going, before you go to some of these
  sessions."
