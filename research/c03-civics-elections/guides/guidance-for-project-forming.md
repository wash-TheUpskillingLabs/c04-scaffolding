# Guidance for Project Forming ("You've Joined a Project - Now what?")

A Civics & Elections guide, as provided by a participant. Names replaced with roles. Layout from the original PDF.

```text
You’ve Joined a Project - Now what?
Guidance for the project forming phase. It’s important to set group expectations up front.

      ​ Hold a Kickoff Meeting. Bring everyone together to align on the items that follow, build
        rapport, and answer open questions before work begins.
      ​ Define Goals & Scope. Clarify the project's purpose, desired outcomes, and
        boundaries. This often takes the form of a team charter or mission.
      ​ Identify Roles & Responsibilities. Assign clear ownership for tasks. Roles to consider
        include: project manager, communication lead, social media lead (responsible for
        external facing materials like LinkedIn), researcher, back end developer, front end
        developer, etc.
      ​ Create a Project Plan. Break the work into tasks with realistic deadlines.
      ​ Establish Norms. Decide how and how often the team will communicate — meeting
        cadence, preferred channels (Slack, email, etc.), and response time expectations. Note:
        Slack is the primary way that the Upskilling Labs communicates with projects.
      ​ Set Decision-Making Processes. Agree on how decisions will be made (consensus,
        majority vote, designated lead) to avoid conflict later.
      ​ Align on Tools & Workflows. Choose shared tools for documents, version control, and
        project tracking so everyone works from the same system.
      ​ Establish a Feedback & Check-in Rhythm. Schedule regular check-ins to track
        progress, surface blockers, and recalibrate as needed.
      ​ Determine the minimum hours per week the team expects each member to commit to
        the project.
      ​ Document Everything. Keep a record of decisions, assignments, and meeting notes so
        nothing falls through the cracks.

Other items to consider.
      ​ This is messy work.
      ​ Your goal is a minimally viable product (MVP) as determined by the team.
      ​ What you present at the final Summit event might not be perfect but it will still be
        amazing!
      ​ Have fun.
      ​ Stretch yourselves. This is about Upskilling in a safe place. If you’ve wanted to try
        something new–do it.

Once you’ve done this, you’re ready for this :
  Guidance for Project Research, Design & getting to Development
```
