# Record: steering committee member's follow-up notes on modules, quality and bottom-up creation

Filed as issue #40. Follows #25 (Nov 5).

1. What it is
A steering committee member's continued thinking on how modules, workshops, quality and contribution should work, in three sets of notes and one clarifying answer, all from Nov 14, 2026. It adds to #25.

2. Where it came from
A steering committee member, identified as an organizer. The pod lead typed the notes into Claude as the member spoke and asked the clarifying question, so these are first-hand notes, not a transcript.

3. When
November 14, 2026.

4. What it says

**What "the workshop model doesn't work" meant (clarifying answer)**

- Workshops rely entirely on outside experts and their own content, with no QA whatsoever.

**A catalog, and who makes it**

- This is a catalog, not a curriculum, since a curriculum is regulated.
- Until a catalog exists, which modules get created depends on a three-sided marketplace: does the organization have interest in putting them together, are experts contributing in any meaningful way, and are participants interested.
- Idea: engage small and mid-sized businesses to tap their experts, develop a first module with them, then anonymize it.
- A roadmap of communities to contribute modules and share knowledge.
- The organization uses Vercel, Supabase and Claude for contribution purposes, which drives its interest in developing modules.

**Structure that emerges, with some top-down parts**

- How can things emerge and be tagged and categorized without the Labs dictating it? Over time a structure will emerge: an adaptive structure.
- Some things are top down. GitHub training is validated as needed to join the ecosystem fully. A tool like Lovable is optional. (Secondhand: a tool vendor pays a person who might want to teach it.)

**Platform, brand and governance (open questions)**

- Unifying frame: is the Labs more of a platform? Are there verified creators on it, or does the Labs consider itself the creator?
- Brand safety, using an analogy of ads on Facebook (not associated with the creator) versus YouTube (much more brand safety). The note on use as a publisher is incomplete.
- How to think about the Labs as a brand. Example: a workshop people didn't love. It should be easy enough for an outside host to run a workshop with feedback and join a community of getting better at workshops.
- Quality with a lean core while distributing across the country. Brand architecture matters, whether the event is a training, hackathon, meetup or workshop, hosted by the Labs or by someone else.
- Can an outside host register an event? Does it need approval? What is the governance? Hyperscale would require making it as easy as possible. The range runs from Luma-easy to as structured as a university (an adjunct professor registering a class).

**What a module is**

- A module is a bundle of content on a topic, for example prompt engineering or rapid prototyping: a blog post, guides, facilitation material, recordings, slide decks.
- A Markdown file is the simplest possible thing to put it in.
- Some modules assume prior skills. Rapid prototyping assumes the learner already knows Git, Vercel and Supabase. It covers iterative prototyping, split and A/B testing, creating prototypes, isolating what you will test, and core ideas.
- Cohort-specific modules: setting up as a team, advice for teams, principles such as team formation, and tool-specific guides. Open: are people allowed to use other coding agents, or only Claude, or is it open as in the past? C04 is Claude.
- Standard organizing structure for content: a Wikipedia-type entry of general knowledge plus resources. If you are a facilitator, here are the resources.
- Core questions: are there specific exercises participants should go through, and what learning outcome is meant to be achieved? Work backwards from there.
- An event's card or thumbnail is its OpenGraph image listing.

**Bottom-up creation**

- Assumption: everything starts hypersituated, tied to one specific situation, for example a participant struggling with the GitHub connector, or a person meeting the Front Door pod about a specific problem.
- Durable principles that hold across situations will emerge over time by abstraction. Log the specific cases and seed modules around them. An example session: "Intro to Claude in context."
- Structure creates opportunity for emerging sessions that can be tailored to specific situations.
- If a team in OLOS escalates and a mentor accepts, how is the way it was navigated captured? Accrue those and create the catalog as the Labs formalizes and maintains it.
- The specific differentiator is bottom-up creation, across regions and types of problems, toward an atlas of skills, future work skills and tools.
- Informal capture: what was the problem, and what worked. The question is how to capture ad-hoc knowledge transfers and skill transfers so they inform formal content and the design and creation of programs.
