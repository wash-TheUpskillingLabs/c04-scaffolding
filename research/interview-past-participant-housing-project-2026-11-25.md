# Interview: Cycle 3 participant on getting stuck in a pod, tool choices, the hackathon tracks, the Learning Log and life after a Cycle

[check: file as a GitHub issue and add the number here]

1. What it is
A conversation about their Cycle: why they came, the project they built,
how their team worked and got unstuck, choosing tools, workshops and the
hackathon, the Learning Log, and what they'd want after a Cycle ends.

2. Where it came from
A Cycle 3 (C03) participant, the same Cycle as the interviewer [check].
They have no tech background ("I don't have a tech background") and work
for themselves. They built and launched a website that helps DC residents
find housing resources, and are still building it.

How they differ from earlier interviewees: they came in through a library
event, not a job search; they were their team's main builder; and they
launched their project themselves and kept going after the Cycle.

3. When
Nov 25, 2026 (about an hour, by video). Quotes are from the transcript.
Two AI meeting summaries (Fathom and Zoom) were compared and not used as
sources. They got at least two things wrong: Fathom says the interviewee
missed the problem-solving workshop at the hackathon (they attended it;
the interviewer was the one in the other room), and both summaries call
ideas "agreements" or "decisions."

4. What it says
(Quotes are verbatim from the transcript, lightly trimmed with "...", with
filler words such as "like" and "you know" removed where they don't change
the meaning. Names of people are replaced with roles.)

How they came in
- "I was at the library, and we were having an event there, and I got
  introduced... [an organizer] gave me the information, and I just started
  to show up." "I was already trying to learn how to use AI tools in my
  life."
- What they hoped for: "I didn't know what I hoped to get out of it,
  because I didn't know what it was."
- The problem was their own: after trouble with a landlord, "I found
  myself... [finding] it very hard to get resources and just finding
  somewhere else to live... I said, okay, well, I'm having this issue, let
  me see where this goes."

What they built, and where it is now
- The site is live. "My ultimate goal is not to just have a website that
  people can go to and learn a little bit and find things easier, but I'm
  trying to actually solve a problem." It's "at an infant stage now."
- How close the Cycle got them: "very close, because... without it, I
  don't even know if I would have built it."
- Since then: talking with council members, housing officials and their
  ANC, and speaking at a roundtable. "It became very clear that I don't
  want to necessarily be doing all of this for free."
- To a hiring manager: "I learned how to pivot critical thinking with
  using AI tools, but not just using the tools, with also learning how to
  work out my problem. So that when I'm using the tool, it is actually
  giving me the thing that I need."
- Launch: "even to just launching the website by myself, I did that by
  myself... I just asked Claude and ChatGPT to help me. I had to take a lot
  of screenshots of stuff and get things explained to me and I got it
  done."

"The chaos makes for the magic"
- "I was kind of told once I got in the group that... it was kind of made
  to have you in a real world scenario to... figure out how to fix these
  problems with a group with not... maybe not much support." (Who told
  them is not said; see section 6.)
- "That's kind of how I've been navigating my whole life, in a way, but
  I don't really like that."
- "But I do understand how those conditions, like the chaos, makes for the
  magic. It gets you to get in there and to really see what is working,
  what's not working."
- Before joining, they looked for what The Labs was and found a recorded
  roundtable online. "I was trying to wrap my head [around], well, what am
  I going to be doing? ... I'm looking at the flyers, I'm reading the
  stuff... what does that look like?"

What was clear
- The weekly schedule: "very straightforward. That was very structured."
  "There was a template that you all gave, and we had to follow that
  recipe to get to the next steps... That's how I ended up getting to
  where I got."
- Tools: Slack, OLOS and Luma were explained at the start, though "I don't
  think everyone probably knew to [go on] Luma."
- But "maybe some of the support around it just needs a little bit
  more."

The hackathon, then the pod
- At the hackathon a mentor introduced them to Lovable and they built a
  first site, then "completely just took all the information I was
  collecting and put it into the website."
- "I didn't know what to expect at the hackathon... I just know that it
  was all day." They'd want each presenter's topic up front, "because it
  may not be relevant to what you're building."
- On pods: "I knew that we were going to have [pods]... But I didn't know
  what that was gonna look like... I had already built my website, and
  some other people had already had projects... Maybe the pod should be
  before the hackathon, so that the groups could be formed." (Their idea.)

The hackathon's two rooms
- The hackathon split people by whether they'd come the Saturday before.
  The interviewer stayed in one room (GitHub setup with a mentor); the
  interviewee was in the other, with three presenters, including the
  problem-solving workshop.
- "I think that was probably not helpful... If we're doing this all as a
  whole collective, we need to all be able to experience those."
- The interviewer noted the same workshops run at other times, often in
  person at 4:30 across town.

Getting stuck in the pod
- "I felt a lot of value in my group because they had a lot of
  experiences that I didn't have... but their availability to what I was
  doing, I felt like, wasn't really there."
- "I got really stuck almost for a week because I was kind of hoping that
  the people in the group did the thing and that kind of didn't work."
- Why teammates held back, as they guessed it: "they may have not just had
  time, or they may have been mad because their thing didn't get picked."
  And: "you guys had built your thing too, and now you are not doing your
  thing, you're doing my thing."
- How they got unstuck: "walking away from it for about 4 days. I didn't
  do anything. And then we had a meeting with the group... that Sunday,"
  and a teammate "really kind of helped me... breathe a little bit life
  back into it."
- "Even some of the people in the group were just saying they needed more
  structure." "All of us were saying we didn't have enough structure."
- "If this hadn't been that important to me, I probably would have needed
  more structure."
- "I was wearing many hats that I didn't really want to wear."

Roles and follow-through
- Their Poderator "did say that we need to pick... people who are gonna be
  in charge of certain things. And we did that, but... someone ended up not
  even being in the group when we presented."
- "In the beginning, just saying who's going to do what and how we
  structured it was amazing. But the actual follow-through on actually
  doing it..."
- What helped: teammates suggested recording meetings "so that we can go
  back and reference things."
- What they'd want: "stronger guidelines about when you do form a group,
  people have to make sure who's wearing what hat, and you have to be
  accountable to help, so people aren't feeling like they're carrying
  everything." And a way out: "if this is not for you, you can just say you
  don't want to be in the group."
- The interviewer summed it up as "ground rules about what you're
  committing to... and maybe some best practices as to how successful
  teams might operate." They agreed: "Yes, yes, yes."
- The missing piece, in their words: "a template or a tool to say, you
  need to be doing this at this time, or at this time, if you're still
  stuck with this, you need to go back to this." They tried applying the
  problem-solving workshop to the team's structure.

Choosing tools: Lovable, ChatGPT, Claude
- "I did it in Lovable. And then they were screaming about Claude... I
  actually had to put it in ChatGPT because I thought that would be easier,
  but they were like, no, we don't like that either, so I did it in
  Claude, and I took a class."
- Lovable cost: a mentor got Lovable credits at a workshop and passed them
  on. Their understanding: The Labs doesn't get educational discounts the
  way schools do, so discounts come through mentors. (Hearsay; see
  section 6.)
- After they rebuilt in Claude, teammates said they liked Lovable.
- They see Lovable as having helped: "I think Lovable helped Claude kind
  of make it a thing for me, because before, Claude was not coming up with
  that type of thing." The site is still in Claude.
- "Through all that trial and error, it taught me something, so I was a
  little heated at the time... Now, someone else going through this with
  not my frame looking at it may have not been so happy."

GitHub, alone and as a team
- "GitHub was, oh my God, like, what is that?"
- Before the hackathon, a mentor set it up for them in person: "I kind of
  just had [him] just do it for me in person, because I didn't know what
  I was doing." After the pod formed: "you can't keep getting [him] to do
  that... so I had to get the group, and we had to have a meeting with
  him."
- Working on code as a team: "thank God that I was the main builder,
  because that would have been a complete nightmare." Claude was connected
  to GitHub; teammates commented, and some sent PDFs. "I rebuilt my site a
  couple of times, just to have different ones, so that they can make
  branches of their own... because I was trying to figure out how to get
  them more engaged."
- Technical, or team dynamics? "Both."
- "It taught me how important saving and backing up all of this stuff
  really is, because... I just kept changing the thing and forgotten to
  back it up."

What would have helped
- Asked what resource would have helped with tool decisions: "More
  instructions and some more guidance and information." "What tool set to
  use when and how."
- On-demand video: "maybe there can be pre-recorded videos that just
  genuinely shows people how to connect your things to GitHub... I don't
  have to be in the Zoom with you... I can go to the website."
- "Some people would like to read stuff, some people like to have visual."
  A "little handbook," and on the website "this is what we do, this is how
  we started, and also this is what these cohorts will look like... a
  reference of what you've already done. 'Cause I was looking for that."
- "If I want to work on something at 3 o'clock in the morning, and I don't
  want to be hitting up people on Slack? Is there somewhere I can go?"
- Recordings of virtual sessions: "can the virtual videos be accessed
  later?" Where they'd look: "YouTube... I do not like apps and portals,
  but I do understand why they're..." Perhaps "a video log" on the website
  or a YouTube channel.
- Before or after an event: "Yes, definitely... it would be good if before
  there's some type of information available and then probably after as
  well."
- The interviewer suggested saying who a session is for and what you get
  out of it. They agreed.
- On freedom: "I like the fact that you guys don't really... do too much
  hand-holding... I love that because I have my own life too... But I do
  understand with that sometimes people do need to be told that you have
  to do something."

Workshops
- "I learned quite a bit of things here from a lot of the presentations...
  They were very thought-provoking, and they gave you clear... structure and
  steps to kind of apply these things."
- They named four presenters, including the problem-solving workshop and
  the storytelling session, and the mentor who helped with GitHub ("you
  can't have this GitHub thing without that").
- The problem-solving workshop at the hackathon: "I apply that to most of
  the things that I do." Still, now. (Title not remembered: "The pain or
  something like that.")

The Learning Log
- "I liked it."
- "If I have to leave and go back, I think all of this stuff erases. And
  you can't—you have to save it all at one time."
- "I was still getting things that said I hadn't filled out my learning
  log when I did... I had 2 dates on there." "It might have been me," they
  added. The interviewer called it a likely bug.
- Did they report being stuck? "I did, I did, I did, yes." Did anyone
  reach out? "I don't think so. No."
- "If someone is reading the logs... for them to reach out and say, hey,
  where are you feeling stuck? What do you need? Maybe someone could have
  reached out to me and my team members individually and maybe got us
  together... because I didn't want to keep feel like I was harassing
  them."
- The interviewer asked about a lightweight, shared note of "we were stuck
  here... and this is what worked." "Yeah, definitely."

After the Cycle
- They're still in touch with some teammates and still come to workshops,
  "networking with people and finding support."
- What they'd want for alumni: "How can I launch? ... how to look for
  investors or how to... scale it... if I'm new to this, then I may not
  have that infrastructure, that framework to say, hey, you built
  something, you have these options."
- User research: "I need to figure out how to take surveys or take...
  some type of questionnaire to find out what I need to improve." The
  interviewer named it "user research"; "Yeah, exactly."
- They didn't join the internal Cycle ("I have a lot on my plate") but
  offered to answer questions and review the interviewer's structure.

Young people and schools
- They asked whether The Labs can't work with teenagers because of age
  limits, from something an organizer said. The interviewer didn't know.
  (Hearsay; see section 6.)
- A DC STEM public high school (named in the interview): "there could be
  a better way to connect teenagers, especially ones about to graduate,
  with tech and AI in a different way." "I would love to facilitate that."
  Possibly with "educational certificate money."

Ideas parked for later (theirs, not evidence; for frames after the paradox)
- Form pods before the hackathon.
- One room at the hackathon for content everyone needs; say what each
  presenter will cover.
- Ground rules, roles and commitments for pods, and a graceful way to
  leave a group.
- A template that says what to do when, and where to go back to when
  stuck.
- Pre-recorded tool videos (GitHub first), recordings of virtual
  sessions, written guides, a handbook, examples of past work, all on the
  website or a YouTube channel.
- Information before and after events.
- Someone reads the Learning Log and reaches out, to the person and the
  team.
- An alumni path: launching, scaling, investors, user research.
- A partnership with a STEM high school.

Interviewer's contributions (kept separate)

- Said they were a Cycle 3 participant, not an organizer, "so don't feel
