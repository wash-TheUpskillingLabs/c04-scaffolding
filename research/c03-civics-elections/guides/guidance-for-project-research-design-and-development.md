Overview

The Design Thinking Framework

When we make projects, programs and products, we typically make them to serve others. Doing
that well requires "design thinking" — a structured but flexible way of understanding a problem
before you build a solution. At a glance, the process looks like this:

   1.​ Define the problem with specificity.
   2.​ Determine who this is for — who is the audience? who are the stakeholders?
   3.​ Describe their pain points.
   4.​ Ask "How might we…?" questions.
   5.​ Brainstorm! Every idea should be put on the table without judgement.
   6.​ Narrow it down. Select solutions based on constraints (e.g.
       time/money/scope/expertise).
   7.​ Prototype, wireframe, or mock up solutions. Remember, these are things that can be
       tested and abandoned quickly.
   8.​ Test. Real users will tell you if your solution is working.
   9.​ Iterate. Be ready to try again.
   10.​Develop on what works! Rinse and repeat the above.

The sections below walk through this same process in more depth.

Audience Research for Design
When your team is newly formed, we suggest:

   ●​ Get to know your audience through surveys and interviews.
   ●​ Perform a competitive analysis of what exists already, and what works or doesn't work
      about it.
   ●​ Look for publicly available data you can use to inform your design and development.

Turning Ideas into Solutions

With the above information:

   ●​ Describe the pain points the audience encounters.
   ●​ Develop "How might we…?" questions. Here's a great resource on how to do this.
   ●​ Brainstorm solutions together as a team. Invite all possibilities without judgement —
      everything should be on the table. Timebox your brainstorms to make them more
      effective; this should feel fun.
   ●​ Narrow it down: of the brainstormed ideas, which feel the most interesting - and viable?
          ○​ You can evaluate them using one of the many Product prioritization frameworks
              available.

Time to Develop a Solution

Now it's time to develop. Many of these steps can be accelerated with AI tools — for example,
asking an AI assistant to draft a first pass of your PRD or use cases, then refining it yourself:

   ●​ Create your Product Requirements Document (PRD).
   ●​ Articulate the feature specifications.
   ●​ Create use cases describing how the user might interact with what you're building.
   ●​ Begin building your prototype, wireframe, and/or mockup — remember, these can be
      tested and abandoned quickly.
   ●​ Start testing with actual users and gather their feedback.
   ●​ Iterate! Take what you've learned and either improve the current project, or start a new
      one with what you've learned.
