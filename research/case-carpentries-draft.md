# Case (draft): The Carpentries

**Status: draft, sources gathered, not yet read.** Gathered Nov 26, 2026,
from search summaries. Check every figure at the source. The builder's
summary (what they tried, what happened, why) is still to come.

Why this case (the builder's reasons): to see how a volunteer-run program
trains and supports its volunteers, since The Carpentries trains volunteer
instructors; and because it keeps its lessons as shared modules on GitHub,
which is close to The Labs' mentors and the module catalog prototype. It
may help with the question #29 and #39 raise for The Labs: how a
volunteer-run program defines, trains and supports its volunteers.

## Who they are

A nonprofit that teaches researchers foundational coding and data skills
through short workshops. It has lesson programs (Software Carpentry, Data
Carpentry, Library Carpentry, and HPC Carpentry, approved in 2026).
Instructors are volunteers "certified through the Carpentries Instructor
Training program" ([instructor handbook](https://github.com/carpentries/docs.carpentries.org/blob/main/source/handbooks/instructors.md)).

## What they tried (sources to read)

**How volunteers become instructors**
- Instructor Training, then a "checkout" of three short tasks, to be
  completed within 90 days (extensions up to a year). (Search summary.)
- [Instructor handbook](https://github.com/carpentries/docs.carpentries.org/blob/main/source/handbooks/instructors.md)
- [The Carpentries: How We Operate](https://carpentries.github.io/instructor-training/15-carpentries)
  (part of the instructor training curriculum)

**How lessons are made and kept**
- Lessons maintained openly on GitHub by a community. (To read: how
  maintainers are chosen and supported.)

**How volunteers are supported** (the gap)
- Blog posts tagged "Mentoring" in 2016 suggest a mentoring program for
  instructors; nothing found yet on what it was or how it went.

## What happened (sources to read)

- Long-term survey, every six months since 2017: the 2017 analysis
  reported "increased confidence and self-efficacy"; the 2020 analysis
  found 66% of respondents had started using a programming language or
  the command line after a workshop. (Search summaries; self-selected
  respondents.)
  [2017 analysis](https://zenodo.org/records/1039944),
  [2020 report](https://zenodo.org/records/3753528/files/2020-01-long-term-report.pdf),
  [assessment page](https://carpentries.org/assessment),
  [impact page](https://carpentries.org/about-us/impact/)
- An outside study: [PLOS One, 2020](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7343163),
  on introductory programming workshops and biomedical researchers'
  workflows.
