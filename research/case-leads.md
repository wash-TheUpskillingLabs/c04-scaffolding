# Case leads

Organizations and programs that might make good case studies, found while
researching the [Code for America Brigades case](case-code-for-america-brigades.md).
None of these has been read closely yet. Each line says what kind of case
it is, because extremes, failures, cases very different from us, and cases
everyone points to teach more than typical ones.

To take one on, open a `Case:` issue or start a file in `research/`, and
note it here.

Collected Nov 24, 2026. Facts below come from search summaries unless
marked otherwise; check them at the source.

## Other programs that teach by doing

| Lead | Kind | Why look | Starting sources |
|---|---|---|---|
| **YouthBuild** | The one everyone points to | Federally funded, cohort-based workforce program; about 247 US programs (2024 fact sheet). The only lead with a randomized national evaluation. An interviewee called it "a strong structural analog." **Unconfirmed:** a 5% technical assistance set-aside written into law. The statute requires DOL to fund technical assistance but no percentage was found. | [MDRC four-year results](https://mdrc.org/publication/laying-foundation/file-executive), [MDRC implementation report](https://mdrc.org/work/publications/adapting-local-context/file-full), [fact sheet](https://youthbuild.org/wp-content/uploads/2024/10/YouthBuild-Fact-Sheet-October-2024.pdf), [29 U.S.C. §3226](https://uscode.house.gov/us/usc/t29/s3226/) |
| **Recurse Center** | Explicit about structure | Self-directed programming retreat: "self-directed does not mean unstructured." Most structure is optional and run by participants. Wrote down its implicit principles as "self-directives" in 2022. Sources are mostly its own. | [Self-directives post](https://assets.recurse.com/blog/167-developing-the-self-directives), [FAQ](https://www.recurse.com/faq), [manual](https://recurse.com/manual) |
| **Fab Academy / Fab Lab network** | Different from us | A global curriculum run through local labs with local peers and mentors; progress by demonstrated skill, not time. Little outcome data found. | [About](https://fabacademy.org/about/), [MIT](https://fab.cba.mit.edu/about/academy) |
| **École 42** | Extreme (no teachers) | Peer-graded projects, no instructors; a month-long entry trial filters hard. No dropout data found. | [WEF](https://www.weforum.org/stories/education-and-skills/welcome-to-ecole-42-the-teacher-less-coding-school-in-paris/), [CACM](https://cacm.acm.org/news/will-ecole-42-crack-the-code-of-learning) |
| **Sudbury Valley School** | Extreme (no imposed structure) | Democratic school with no learning requirements. Graduate follow-ups are small, self-reported and partly school-run. | [Gray & Chanoff, 1986](https://www.self-directed.org/resource/gray-and-chanoff-1986), [Hudson Valley survey](https://cdn3.psychologytoday.com/assets/hvss_study_published.pdf) |
| **Dev Bootcamp** | Failure | Immersive coding bootcamp, about 3,000 graduates; closed in 2017, never profitable. Coverage is about the business, not the teaching. | [EdSurge](https://edsurge.com/news/2017-07-12-unable-to-reach-a-sustainable-business-model-dev-bootcamp-will-shut-down-in-december), [Quartz](https://qz.com/1038508/dev-bootcamps-president-explains-why-the-coding-bootcamp-shut-down) |
| **Mozilla Open Leaders** | Close to The Labs in design | Cohorts with 1:1 mentoring every two weeks; mentors were past participants; a later round trained graduates to run their own programs. No evaluation found. | [Mozilla](https://www.mozillafoundation.org/en/initiatives/mozilla-open-leaders/), [2016 pilot](https://github.com/zee-moz/mentorship-program) |
| **Civic Hall** (New York) | Not yet assessed | Raised by the builder: training, cycles, hackathons. | *None yet* |
| **Code for All** | Offshoot of the Brigade model | International network that grew from the Brigade model in 2013; first run by Code for America, later member-run. | [History](https://medium.com/code-for-all/how-this-thing-started-code-for-alls-journey-from-creation-to-future-visions-6d45fb8434), [about](https://codeforall.org/about-us) |

## Open labs and makerspaces

| Lead | Kind | Why look | Starting sources |
|---|---|---|---|
| **The Clubhouse Network** (formerly Computer Clubhouse, from 1993) | The one everyone points to; long-running | Free after-school tech labs for young people; over 100 sites in 20 countries. Group mentoring: each young person works with several adults matched by interest. Evaluations found sites differed mainly by local resources, such as the coordinator's experience and the supply of volunteer mentors. Evidence mostly from the 2000s; latest independent review 2017. | [MIT Media Lab](https://www-prod.media.mit.edu/projects/computer-clubhouse/overview/), [evaluation summary](https://peertje.daanberg.net/drivers/intel/download.intel.com/education/EvidenceOfImpact/EvaluationResourceDocuments/7_Intel_Computer_Clubhouse_Network/Clubhouse_EvalSummary.pdf), [NSF profile](https://paesmem.nsf.gov/paesmemRecognition/awardeeProfile/1170) |
| **YOUmedia** (Chicago Public Library) | Explicit about structure | Library digital media space for teens, designed around HOMAGO (hanging out, messing around, geeking out): three levels of involvement each teen chooses. Three-year implementation study (2013). | [HOMAGO](https://youmedia.org/?p=180), [UChicago study](https://ccsr.uchicago.edu/sites/default/files/2023-06/Teens%20Digital%20Media-May2013-Consortium.pdf) |
| **IMLS / MacArthur Learning Labs** | A replication | 24 labs in libraries and museums modeled on YOUmedia, funded from 2011. Teens were directly involved in designing the spaces. | [2014 report](https://imls.gov/sites/default/files/publications/documents/learninglabsreport_0.pdf), [summary](https://www.imls.gov/sites/default/files/legacy/assets/1/AssetManager/LearningLabsLExecutiveSummary.pdf) |
| **Noisebridge** (San Francisco hackerspace) | Extreme (almost no imposed structure) | Run by "do-ocracy" (whoever does the work decides), with formal consensus only for big changes. Near-collapse around 2011; a pattern noted across hackerspaces of fewer members doing the work and leaders picking up the slack. Sources mostly its own wiki and mailing lists. | [Do-ocracy](https://www.noisebridge.net/wiki/Do-ocracy), [Hackaday, 2019](https://hackaday.com/2019/12/13/reflection-on-a-decade-of-hackerspace-expansion), [Mission Local, 2023](https://missionlocal.org/2023/04/noisebridge-the-last-free-space-in-san-francisco/) |

## Adults

| Lead | Kind | Why look | Starting sources |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Extension Master Gardener** | Long-running; named in the README (Cooperative Extension) | Adults get about 40 hours of training, then give about 40 volunteer hours back; in all 50 states since 1996; began in Washington State in 1973. Learners become volunteers on a fixed exchange. No comparative evaluation found yet. **Status: draft with sources, summary not yet written ([case-master-gardener-draft.md](case-master-gardener-draft.md)).** | [Nebraska, 50 years](https://newsroom.unl.edu/announce/lancasterextension/20293/108100), [Illinois evaluation guide](https://extension.illinois.edu/pre/master-gardener-programs) |
| **Per Scholas** (Bronx, WorkAdvance study) | The one everyone points to; randomized evaluation | Free IT training for adults who were unemployed or earning under $15 an hour. 349 offered the program, 341 control. Earnings up 14% in year 7; the only WorkAdvance site with a significant year-7 gain. | [MDRC seven-year report](https://mdrc.org/work/publications/employment-and-earnings-effects-workadvance-demonstration-after-seven-years/file-full), [DOL CLEAR](https://clear.dol.gov/Study/Employment-and-earnings-effects-WorkAdvance-demonstration-after-seven-years-Kanengiser-et-al) |
| **MOOCs** (edX, Harvard and MIT) | Extreme (content, little support); failure on completion | Completion "has not budged in six years," even among learners who intended to finish (Reich & Ruipérez-Valiente, *Science*, 2019). | [MIT summary](https://tsl.mit.edu/research/the-mooc-pivot/), [postprint](https://webs.um.es/jruiperez/papers/journals/2019_Science_MOOCPivot_postprint.pdf) |
| **Senior Planet from AARP** | AI era; different from us | Free tech classes for adults 60+, now including AI literacy; an "AI Skills Jam" in 10 cities with OpenAI as partner. AARP says nearly 800,000 people a year. No evaluation found. | [Senior Planet AI](https://seniorplanet.org/ai), [OpenAI announcement](https://openai.com/index/helping-older-adults-use-ai-in-everyday-life/), [CBS Colorado](https://www.cbsnews.com/colorado/news/aarp-senior-planet-program-older-adults-smartphones) |

## Collaborative learning, build cycles and open upskilling

| Lead | Kind | Why look | Starting sources |
|---|---|---|---|
| **P2PU learning circles** (Peer 2 Peer University) | Very close to The Labs; adults; peer-led | Groups of 5–15 adults meet weekly in public libraries to work through an online course, led by a non-expert facilitator (often a librarian or volunteer). A 2018 University of Washington survey: at least three-quarters of respondents met their goals (about 15% responded). Libraries short on staff time tested volunteer-led circles. **Builder: strong contender.** | [UW report](https://digital.lib.washington.edu/researchworks/items/bac0a618-d298-4afc-bc60-ff33e26b2d9d), [TASCHA summary](https://tascha.uw.edu/new-tascha-report-summarizes-data-from-p2pu-survey), [MIT Media Lab](https://www-prod.media.mit.edu/projects/peer-2-peer-university/overview) |
| **Pursuit "AI-Native" cohorts** (New York City) | AI era; adults; build cohorts | Adult workforce program rebuilt around AI in 2025; participants are called "Builders." A 7-month AI program replaced a year-long coding fellowship. Over 325 Builders in three 2025 cohorts, about 1,000 hours each building with AI (Pursuit's own figures). No independent outcomes yet; earnings claims describe the older program. **Status: written ([case-pursuit.md](case-pursuit.md)).** | [2025 review](https://www.pursuit.org/blog/2025-builds), [AI-Native pilot](https://www.pursuit.org/blog/ai-native-pilot-launch), [Chronicle of Higher Ed](https://www.chronicle.com/featured/student-success/proof-of-work-pursuit), [directory caveat](https://www.techinterview.org/bootcamp_directory/p/pursuit/) |
| **The Carpentries** | Close to The Labs in design; volunteer-run; long-running | Short hands-on workshops on coding and data skills taught by trained volunteer instructors, with lessons maintained openly on GitHub by a community. Runs a long-term learner survey every six months since 2017: the 2017 analysis reported "increased confidence and self-efficacy"; a 2020 analysis found 66% of respondents had started using a programming language or the command line after a workshop. Survey data and reports are public. Little found yet on how volunteer instructors are supported. **Status: draft with sources ([case-carpentries-draft.md](case-carpentries-draft.md)).** | [Assessment](https://carpentries.org/assessment), [impact](https://carpentries.org/about-us/impact/), [2020 long-term report](https://zenodo.org/records/3753528/files/2020-01-long-term-report.pdf), [2017 analysis](https://zenodo.org/records/1039944), [PLOS One, 2020](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7343163) |
| **The Odin Project** | Extreme open upskilling | Free, open-source web development curriculum with a volunteer-run Discord. No cohorts, deadlines or certificate; a portfolio is the proof. No completion data. | [About](https://www.theodinproject.com/about), [FAQ](https://www.theodinproject.com/faq) |
